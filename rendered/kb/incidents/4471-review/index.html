The published counts come from the supplied uniqueById, groupByCause, causeOf, tenantOf, and readAs logic. The export has overlapping pages: count each report ID once, retaining its last occurrence, and filter to mergedInto === 4471. Identical repeats do not represent extra reports.

Cause follows the tenant’s locale, booking-form version and report channel. The browser locale and intake label are not authoritative. Determine day-first ordering with Intl.DateTimeFormat(...).formatToParts, as the supplied code does; do not maintain a guessed locale list.

Distinct problems in the historical reports

  • Parser: the shared parser reads month-first although the tenant writes day-first. This is the majority cause belonging to the original incident. Its corrective contract is day-first parsing, with regression coverage for day-first app/email paths and booking-v2 forms; the later desk update reports the parser fixed. See parser issue.
  • Hint: booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but its form reads the tenant’s day-first format. The hint and interpretation needed to agree. The desk retired booking-v3 rather than fixing this hint; a parser-only fix must not be mistaken for a hint fix. See hint issue.

The original cause table gave the hint group no ticket of its own. The exported incident’s statement that there was one issue and one queued fix was therefore not supported by its own rendered evidence. Separate public issues now preserve both causes and their different reported closure reasons.

Symptoms are not proven root causes

The desk read some reports as a one-day move, while the classifier files them under parser. The table preserves that count within parser, not in addition to it. This discrepancy merits investigation of expected/actual dates and timezone handling; it does not prove a third defect or justify changing the classifier’s totals.

Queue history is not arrival history

Reports begin in April; merging begins in July. The large merge spike represents consolidation of earlier reports, not an equally large surge of newly received reports. Keep received and merged series separate when describing impact. Reports continue through the export date; “known” is a workflow label, not evidence that impact stopped. The product’s approximate user population is not a measured affected-user count.

Change the desk’s deduplication decision

Subject-only matching is not sufficient. The executable examples demonstrate both different subjects sharing a cause and identical subjects hiding distinct causes; missing tenant context cannot establish equivalence. See the proposed replacement decision.

Preserve accepted decision 0001 unchanged. Read the governing logic and data alongside narrative, check review age, and consult current queue evidence before acting on this snapshot. A new incident page does not itself change the desk’s active workflow.
